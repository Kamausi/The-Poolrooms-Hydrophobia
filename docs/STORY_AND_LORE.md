# UNDERFLOW — Story & Lore Bible

> *"The Backrooms doesn't flood. It drains. And everything that drains has to go somewhere."*

This is the working story bible. Everything here is first-draft canon: it should feel settled enough to build against, but every section can be argued with. Open decisions are listed at the end.

---

## 1. The core idea

The Backrooms is famous for being dry, yellow and endless. People forget the damp carpet. The water has to come from somewhere, and it has to go somewhere.

**The Poolrooms are the Backrooms' drainage layer.** Every damp stain, burst pipe and flooded corridor above slowly seeps down into an enormous network of tiled channels, reservoirs and slides. Whatever falls into that water goes with it: objects, light, sound, and people who wandered too long and stopped being missed.

The title has two meanings:
- **Underflow** is the current *beneath* the overflow: the water you can't see that pulls everything down.
- In computing, **underflow** is a number too small to represent, so it gets *rounded to zero*. That's what happens to people down here. They aren't killed. They're slowly made too small to count, then forgotten.

**The horror isn't drowning. It's being made peaceful enough to stop resisting.**

### Tone rules
1. **Calm is the threat.** The water is warm, the light is soft, the hum is soothing. Fear comes from how good it feels to stay.
2. **It's a ride, and a ride wants riders.** The slides were built *for* you. Someone or something wants you to enjoy them.
3. **Quiet, then chaos.** On foot it's slow, exposed and silent, like Left 4 Dead between hordes. On the raft it's loud, fast and full of targets, like a dark-ride shooter gone wrong.
4. **Body horror, not gore.** Nothing here is torn apart. Bodies *change*: they prune, soften, swell, fold and fuse, slowly and without blood, under clean fluorescent light. The water preserves everything, so what you find is pale, swollen and perfectly clean. See [Body horror](#body-horror) below.

### Body horror
The Poolrooms don't kill people. They *underflow* them. It happens in stages, and every entity is one of those stages gone wrong.

| Stage | What happens to the body |
|---|---|
| **Pruning** | Fingertips wrinkle and stay wrinkled. Days later the whole hand is wrinkled. It never smooths out again. |
| **Softening** | Skin goes white and soft as wet paper. It tears like paper too, painlessly. Tile chips and grout work their way in under it. |
| **Swelling** | Water fills the body. Faces go smooth and featureless. Eyes cloud over and start to reflect, like the surface of a pool. |
| **Folding** | The body gets *smaller*. Joints bend the wrong way to make room. People fold up like letters, tighter and tighter, still breathing. |
| **Rounding to zero** | What's left is too small to count. It goes down the drain. |

Rules for using it:
- **Slow and close, never splatter.** The camera lingers on a hand, not a wound.
- **Clean.** No blood, no rot. The chlorine keeps everything sterile, which is worse.
- **Personal.** The worst body horror happens to people the crew knew. Every special entity was once a person, and late-game graffiti names some of them.
- **The player is not exempt.** As Drift rises, the player's own hands (holding the blaster) start to prune and soften. At full Drift, the fingers have started to fold.

## 2. Why you're here: the Drainers

### The problem above
For as long as anyone can remember, water in the Backrooms has drained away on its own. Lately it hasn't. Corridors that were damp are now ankle-deep. Outposts are flooding. Water stains spread across the ceiling tiles like bruises.

Something down below is **blocked**.

### The Pumphouse
The **Pumphouse** is a survivor outpost built around an old maintenance room in the Backrooms, one of the few places with working machinery. Its people noticed the rising water first. They're the only ones who know there's a way down: a maintenance hatch that opens onto a waterslide.

They send down **Drainer crews**: four-person teams with a salvaged raft and the Arc Blaster. Each crew has three jobs:
1. **Find the blockage** and clear it before the upper levels drown.
2. **Recover the lost.** Crews bring back belongings, and sometimes people.
3. **Map the way back.** Nobody from the last three crews has reported in.

### The personal hook
The previous crew, **Crew Seven**, went down six weeks ago. Their raft came back up the slide empty: still floating, still dry inside, with all four life vests neatly folded on the seat.

Every member of your crew knew someone on Crew Seven.

## 3. The playable crew

Four survivors, Left 4 Dead style. Each is playable, each has an AI-controlled version, and each has a personal thread that pays off by the end. They talk to each other constantly; their banter fills the quiet stretches and warns of threats.

| | Role | Background | Why they went down | What the Poolrooms offers them |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Marisol "Mars" Teague** | Crew lead | Was a lifeguard before she noclipped into the Backrooms. Instinctively counts heads. | Her younger brother **Caleb** was on Crew Seven. | The Lifeguard tower calls to her. Some part of her wants the job back. |
| **Desmond "Dez" Okoro** | Engineer | Pumphouse mechanic. Built the Arc Blaster from fluorescent ballasts. | He designed Crew Seven's raft and needs to know why it came back empty. | The machinery down here is *better* than anything he could build. He wants to understand it. |
| **Tobias "Tubs" Ferrante** | Ride expert / comic relief | Worked as a dark-ride operator at an amusement park in his old life. | He volunteered. He's the only one who understands *ride logic*. | He recognises the slides as a ride system with queues, sensors, a score and a dispatch schedule. He's the first to ask who's operating it. |
| **June Abernathy** | Mapper | Quiet. Keeps the Pumphouse's hand-drawn maps. Has very good hearing. | Crew Seven's mapper, **Priya**, was her partner. | June hears Priya calling "Marco" from deeper down. She's the most at risk from the Marco entity. |

**Design note:** in single-player, you pick one survivor and the other three are AI teammates. Story moments happen whoever you're playing as, but the dialogue changes depending on who's in the party.

## 4. The world: how the Poolrooms work

### Depth is the progression
The deeper you go, the purer and more dangerous the Poolrooms become. Colour shows how deep you are:

| Depth | Palette | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **0–15 m** | Yellow-green tile, stained grout | Still the Backrooms, just wet. Familiar fluorescent buzz. |
| **15–35 m** | White and pale blue | The classic Poolrooms. Bright, sterile, silent. The most beautiful and the most dangerous. |
| **35 m+** | Dark blue to black, lit by underwater lamps | Night swimming. Blacklight targets glow. The pull of the current is strongest. |

This matches the current build's three sectors: **The Yellow Channel** (12 m), **The Blue Reservoir** (28 m) and **The Last Drain** (46 m).

### The paradox
**The way out is down.** The slides only go one way. Every crew that tried to climb back up the slide got nowhere: the slide kept going, the water kept pulling. The rumour among the Lido (below) is that the Main Drain at the very bottom empties into *somewhere else*: a surface, a real world, a way home.

That's why the current ending card reads *"You found the surface."* Whether that's true is the game's final question.

### The Ride
The slides aren't random plumbing. They form **a ride**, with:
- **Dispatch gates** that open on a schedule
- **Targets** built into the walls, which score points when shot
- **Signage**: `CONTAINMENT FAILURE`, `NO EXIT`, `PLEASE REMAIN SEATED`, `RESERVOIR / HIGH FLOW`
- **A score**, the **Signal Score**, that is tracked even if no one is watching

Nobody knows who built it. Tubs's theory: **rides are built to move people efficiently through a space and keep them happy while they wait.** The Poolrooms is moving people down, and the score keeps them happy.

### The Attendant
A voice over a crackling PA system. Polite, warm, a little too slow. You never see it, and it's always "it", never he or she.

> *"Welcome, riders. Please keep your hands and feet inside the raft at all times."*
> *"Excellent score. You may be eligible for an upgrade."*
> *"Attention: the Shallow End is now closed. Please proceed to the Deep End."*

**Gameplay role:** the Attendant is the in-world face of the **AI Director**, the system (borrowed from Left 4 Dead) that controls pacing. When the Attendant announces a closure, the route changes. When it says *"Rush hour,"* a horde is coming. Players learn to read it.

**Lore role:** an "upgrade" means being sent deeper. High scorers get pulled down faster. The game rewards you for playing well and quietly punishes you for it.

**How much is explained:** never in plain dialogue, but there are enough clues to work it out. The Attendant is whoever took the Lifeguard's chair in a previous loop: an earlier Mars. The clues: it knows the crew's names, its pauses match Mars's speech, and a Safe Room message says *check the handwriting*. The secret ending confirms it. Players who find it get the answer; everyone else gets a mystery that holds together.

### The Arc Blaster
Dez's weapon. It's built from fluorescent ballasts and fires the **60 Hz hum** of the lights back at things in concentrated bursts.

**Why it works:** entities down here are made of still water and reflection. A sharp burst of light breaks their surface tension and scatters them. That's why hits read as **"Entity dispersed,"** not "killed."

**Upgrades** (crafted in Safe Rooms) change the frequency, beam spread and charge.

### Signals (the green targets)
The green glowing rings in the current build are **Signals**: beacons painted by earlier crews, charged with light. Shooting one releases a burst of warm, *real* light that restores health. They're breadcrumbs left by people who came before. Late in the game, you start finding Signals with **Crew Seven's** markings.

## 5. Entities

These are new entities, made for the Poolrooms. None of them are taken from existing Backrooms fiction. Following Left 4 Dead's structure, there's one **common** type that comes in swarms, several **specials**, each with a clear tell and a clear counter, and one **stalker** you must avoid rather than fight.

Every entity works in both modes: **on the raft** (rail shooter) and **on foot** (exploration).

### Common: Blooms
*The red spiked orbs in the current build.*

Clots of rust, hair, algae and something that pulses. They drift in the current and swell when they sense warmth.

- **Raft:** float toward you in clusters and burst on contact (damage). Shoot them before they arrive.
- **Foot:** cling to walls and ceilings in dark corners, then drop into the water around you.
- **Tell:** a faint red glow under the surface and the smell of iron.
- **Lore:** Blooms grow wherever someone has *dissolved*. Up close they're hair, fingernails, baby teeth and flakes of soft skin, knotted together with rust and beating with a slow pulse. Big clusters mean someone was lost there. Sometimes a Bloom still wears a ring.

### Special: The Undertow
Never seen directly. It lives under the surface. Its only sign is that **the water goes perfectly still** in a patch, even while it ripples everywhere else.

- **Foot:** grabs the player from below and drags them toward the nearest drain. They can't free themselves; a teammate has to shoot the still patch. *(This is the Left 4 Dead "pinning" role: it punishes anyone who strays from the group.)*
- **Raft:** hooks the raft and pulls it toward a **wrong branch** in the slide. Shoot the still patch before the junction or you're diverted to a harder route.
- **Counter:** stay together; watch for calm water.
- **What it is:** you only see it when it has you. Arms, pale and soft as wet paper, with far too many joints, and webbed fingers that close around the ankle like a cuff. There is no body attached that anyone has found.

### Special: Marco
A voice in the distance calling **"Marco…"** Its voice sounds like someone you know.

- **Mechanic:** if any player answers, by typing or saying "Polo" over voice chat or by pressing the call button, Marco learns where they are. It can also **imitate teammates' voice lines** to lure players away from the group.
- **Foot:** leads players into flooded dead ends. A player who follows it alone starts **Drifting** fast.
- **Raft:** calls from side passages, tempting you to shoot the targets that open those shortcuts. Some shortcuts are real; some lead into it.
- **Counter:** never answer. Stick together. June can tell real voices from fake ones; she's the crew's early warning.
- **Lore:** Marco is not one creature. It's the echo of everyone who called out down here and was never answered. If you find where the voice comes from, it's a throat: just a throat, grown into the tiles at the waterline, swollen with all the voices it has swallowed.

### Special: Lane Rope
A rope of floats strung across a channel. Then it moves.

- **Raft:** stretches across the slide ahead. Shoot the glowing floats to break it before it clotheslines the crew, otherwise someone gets knocked into the water and has to swim to catch up.
- **Foot:** coils around pillars and ladders and wraps around anyone who touches it.
- **Counter:** shoot the floats, which are its weak points. Each one cracks with a sound like a beach ball popping.

### Special: Grout
Something that lives *in the walls*, moving through the lines between tiles. Grout lines darken and crawl as it passes. Tiles fall away where it comes out.

- **Foot:** creates ambushes by sealing doorways shut or opening new ones. It rearranges the layout while you're in it.
- **Raft:** shifts the tiles of the slide itself, opening holes and gaps. Shoot the darkened tile to drive it out.
- **Counter:** watch for spreading dark lines and shoot where they converge.
- **What it is:** people who sat against the walls too long. The tile grew over them. The grout lines are seams of skin. When a tile falls away, something soft and white is underneath, and it is still moving.

### Special: The Floater
Looks like a person lying face-down in the water, perfectly still. Sometimes it is. Sometimes it's a missing Drainer, and you can save them.

- **Mechanic:** walk close and it rolls over and grabs you. But some floaters really are survivors (rescue them for bonus supplies and lore). Players have to decide whether to check.
- **Counter:** a single blaster shot makes a fake floater flinch, but it also scares a real survivor away. The game deliberately keeps this uncertain.
- **What it is:** swollen smooth, skin translucent enough to see the water inside. When it rolls over, its face has no features left, and its eyes are two small circles of pool surface reflecting the ceiling lights.

### Stalker: The Lifeguard
A very tall, thin figure on an impossibly high white chair, overlooking the larger pools. Whistle around its neck. Sunglasses that never move.

- **Rule:** **don't let it see you in the water.** When it spots a swimmer, it **blows its whistle**, and every entity nearby converges on that position. (This combines Left 4 Dead's Witch, which you avoid, with its car alarm, which triggers a horde.)
- **Foot:** move along the dry pool edges, stay behind columns, and use blacklight targets to make flashes that blind it for a few seconds.
- **Raft:** some slide routes run right past its chair. Shoot the overhead lights to blind it as you pass, or brace for a horde.
- **It cannot be killed.** It can only be avoided or blinded.
- **Lore:** the Lifeguard counts heads. It wants everyone *in the water*, where they belong. Mars eventually realises it's counting her crew, and the count is always one too many.
- **What it is:** sunburned skin on a body that has never seen the sun, peeling in long strips. The sunglasses have grown into its face. The whistle has grown into its lips; to blow it, it squeezes its own chest.

### Hazard: Chlorine & Drift
Not an entity but a pressure system that keeps players moving.

- **Chlorine fumes** rise if a crew lingers too long in one area. They blur vision and slowly drain health. This stops players from camping.
- **Drift** replaces the "sanity meter" from the original pitch. In this world the danger isn't panic; it's **peace**. Drift rises while you're in warm water, alone, or listening to Marco or the Attendant. As Drift goes up:
  - The music gets softer and more beautiful.
  - The HUD fades.
  - The Attendant starts speaking *to you directly, by name*.
  - Your character's hands, holding the blaster, start to prune and soften.
  - At full Drift, your character **sits down in the water** and stops responding until a teammate pulls them up. (Solo, you mash Space or fire to snap out of it.)
- Drift drops in Safe Rooms, near teammates, near Signals, and when you shoot (noise and light pull you back).

## 6. Safe Rooms

In the current build, sectors end at a **maintenance dock**: you leave the raft, find a **power cell**, and reach the **green saferoom**. That structure stays.

### What they are
**Plant rooms**: mechanical rooms full of pumps and filters, buried behind thick bulkhead doors. They're the only places in the Poolrooms that are **dry, loud and harshly lit**. The noise of the machinery drowns out Marco. The light is too bright for anything that lives in water.

The power cell restarts the door mechanism. Once the bulkhead seals, nothing gets in.

### What you do there (Left 4 Dead structure)
- **Heal**, and **reset Drift** to zero.
- **Craft** blaster charges and upgrades from parts gathered on foot.
- **Choose the next route.** A dispatch board shows two or three slides, each with its own risks and rewards.
- **Read the walls.** Every Safe Room is covered in **graffiti from earlier crews**: warnings, jokes, tally marks, arguments, goodbye notes. This is one of the main ways the story is told. Messages from Crew Seven get more frequent, and stranger, the deeper you go.

### The Lido
In deeper Safe Rooms you sometimes meet the **Lido**: survivors who stopped trying to leave and live permanently in the plant rooms. They trade, they repair, they know rumours. They are very relaxed. Some of them have high Drift and don't seem to mind.

The Lido believe the Main Drain is **a door home**. Their leader, **Mrs. Vance**, has been down so long she can't remember the colour yellow. Her skin is soft as tissue and she keeps her hands in her lap, folded, because they don't unfold anymore.

## 7. The campaign

Structured like a Left 4 Dead campaign: chapters ending in Safe Rooms, then a finale where the crew has to hold out. Each chapter alternates **raft → dock → on foot → Safe Room**.

### Campaign 1 — "Overflow"
| # | Chapter | Depth | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Yellow Channel** | 12 m | The descent. Blooms only. The Attendant welcomes you. First dock, first power cell, first Safe Room. |
| 2 | **The Blue Reservoir** | 28 m | The classic white-and-blue Poolrooms. First Undertow. Marco is heard for the first time; June goes quiet. |
| 3 | **The Last Drain** | 46 m | Night swimming. Lifeguard towers. Crew Seven's empty campsite and a Signal with Caleb's markings. |

**The current build is a prototype of this campaign.**

### Later campaigns (outline)
- **Campaign 2 — "Deep End":** the Lido, the dispatch board's alternative routes, Grout reshaping a maze of pools. Finale: a holdout at a flooding Safe Room while the Attendant announces "closing time."
- **Campaign 3 — "Night Swim":** total darkness except blacklight targets. The Lifeguard is everywhere. The crew finds Priya, or what Marco made from her voice.
- **Campaign 4 — "The Main Drain":** the bottom.

### The truth about the blockage
At the bottom is the **Main Drain**: a grate the size of a cathedral, with water spiralling through it. It's blocked by a mass of Floaters, Blooms and belongings: everyone who was lost, packed together and calm.

Crew Seven are part of it. They aren't dead. They're **underflowed**: folded small, fused into the mass, too small to count, perfectly at peace. Caleb's face is in there. It smiles when Mars says his name.

### The endings
**Canon ending: "The Loop."** The crew clears the drain. The blockage goes down, Crew Seven with it, and the water drops. The crew climbs a ladder out of the Main Drain, up into yellow light and damp carpet, and finds itself at the top of the Yellow Channel. A raft is waiting: dry, with four life vests folded neatly on the seat. The tag is in Mars's handwriting.

The raft that came back empty at the start of the game was theirs. They are the crew that went down before. The ride ends where it started.

Why this is the canon one:
- It pays off the opening hook (the empty raft) instead of leaving it as flavour.
- It explains the Safe Room graffiti that turns out to be in your own handwriting.
- It fits Tubs's line that every ride ends where it started, and it makes replaying the campaign part of the story.
- The current build already ends this way.

**Alternate ending: "Seal the drain."** The crew closes the drain with Pumphouse equipment. Crew Seven stays below: lost, but whole. The loop breaks, the water keeps rising more slowly, and the crew climbs back the long way. Bittersweet, and the only ending in which nobody goes down.

**Secret ending: "Count heads."** Reached with full Drift at the Main Drain. Mars climbs the Lifeguard's chair and takes the whistle. The ride needs an operator. The credits roll over the Attendant's voice, and for the first time it's Mars's voice, slowed down.

## 8. How the story is told

No long cutscenes. The story comes through:
- **Crew banter** while riding and exploring (Left 4 Dead style)
- **Attendant announcements**
- **Safe Room graffiti**
- **Lost & Found items**: a sunglasses case, a child's armband, a Pumphouse ID badge. Each has a one-line note, collected in an in-game logbook.
- **Field Manual pages** (the build already has *Field Manual / 001*): Pumphouse documents explaining the gear, written by Dez, with increasingly worried notes in the margins.

## 9. Inspirations (not copies)

- **Left 4 Dead:** four-person co-op, special entities with distinct counters, a pacing director, safe rooms with sealed doors and graffiti, campaigns with finales. Underflow borrows the *structure*, not the characters, enemies or story.
- **Six Flags' Boo Blasters on Boo Hill:** dark-ride shooting gallery with targets built into the set and a running score. Underflow borrows the *ride feel*: the raft, the score, the targets that make the scenery react.
- **The Backrooms / Poolrooms:** the community-created liminal-space mythology. Underflow uses the setting, but its entities, factions and characters are original.
